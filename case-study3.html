The Problem

"How might we improve the NISRA mobile page to enhance usability, simplify navigation, and improve accessibility for participants and volunteers?"

NISRA offers year-round recreation programs for individuals with disabilities, but their mobile site was difficult to navigate. Cluttered menus, outdated content, and unclear information architecture made it frustrating for users to register for programs or find volunteer opportunities.

Three Critical Navigation Barriers

1
Confusing Navigation
Cluttered menus with redundant labels and inconsistent terminology made it difficult for users to find key information like programs, forms, and volunteer opportunities.
2
Complex Task Completion
Registering for events or accessing volunteer information required multiple steps through unclear pathways, causing user frustration and task abandonment.
3
Overwhelming Information
Outdated content and unclear categorization created information overload, reducing engagement and making the site feel overwhelming to navigate.

Research Goals & Process

🎯 Research Goals

  • • Understand how participants, caregivers, and volunteers use the mobile site

Research Methods & Key Findings

1. Content Inventory Audit

I conducted a thorough audit of the existing site structure to identify redundancies, outdated content, and information gaps.

Redundant Labels Found
Multiple menu items with similar names (e.g., "Programs", "Programs & Events", "Special Events") created confusion about where to find information.
Inconsistent Terminology
Different sections used varying terms for the same concepts, making navigation unpredictable for users.
Content Gaps Identified
Outdated or missing content for volunteers and program access reduced the site's usefulness for key user groups.

2. Card Sorting (2 Rounds)

I ran two rounds of card sorting with 12 participants to understand user mental models and expectations for menu structure.

Card Sorting Results: Category Agreement

Key Findings from Card Sorting

  • Round 1 (Closed): Users struggled with finding FAQs (58% success), forms (62% success), Special Olympics (54% success), and volunteer sections (63% success)
  • Round 2 (Hybrid): Revised categories helped reduce confusion and increase task success to 74-88% across all categories

3. Contextual Inquiries via Zoom

I observed 10 users (individuals with disabilities, caregivers, and staff) as they navigated the site via Zoom, documenting pain points and behaviors.

"I keep clicking around just to find program info—it's frustrating."

— Participant 1, Contextual Inquiry

Unclear Menu Labels
Users couldn't predict what content would be under menu items like "Resources" or "Community"
Redundant Pages
Some content appeared in multiple locations, while critical information was buried or misplaced
Avoidance Behavior
Users avoided key sections due to outdated or unclear structure, missing important information

User Persona: Alex

👤

Recent college graduate studying graphic design who is passionate about volunteering and giving back to the community.

🎯 Needs and Goals

Alex is looking for volunteer opportunities that align with his interest in working with individuals with disabilities. He wants to easily find volunteer positions and submit applications.

⚠️ Challenges

Alex often faces challenges in identifying volunteer roles that match his skills and availability. The current site structure makes it difficult to find and apply for positions.

Revised Information Architecture

Based on research findings, I created a new sitemap that simplified navigation, removed redundancies, and organized content according to user mental models.

Key Improvements to Site Structure

  • Moved high-traffic items like Summer Camps and Volunteer Roles under more intuitive categories based on user expectations
  • Removed or merged outdated pages and redundant menu items to reduce clutter
  • Simplified terminology across all sections to create consistent, predictable labels
  • Created clear pathways for the two main user tasks: program registration and volunteer applications

Validation Through Testing

Tree Testing Results (2 Rounds)

I tested the new menu structure with 12 participants across 2 rounds to validate improvements and identify any remaining issues.

Tree Testing Success Rates by Task

Task Success Rate Average Time Notes
Find Summer Camps 74% 15 sec Some confusion with category names initially
Volunteer Information 88% 20 sec Greatly improved after label changes
Register for Event 79% 18 sec Some misclicks under "General Info"
Accessibility Resources 92% 12 sec High visibility post-relabeling

First-Click Testing

I measured how quickly users could find key features to validate the intuitiveness of the new structure.

First-Click Success Rates

Key Findings from First-Click Testing

  • 90% success locating Accessibility Resources (up from 62% in round 1)
  • 83% success for Volunteer section (some expected it under "Community" initially)
  • Some initial hesitation around Event Registration tabs, resolved through iteration

Before & After: Key Task Flows

Task 1: Finding & Registering for Summer Camp

❌ BEFORE
  • Summer Camps buried under generic "Programs" section
  • No clear pathway to registration form
  • Users had to navigate through 4-5 pages
  • High abandonment rate (68% didn't complete)
✅ AFTER
  • Summer Camps prominently featured in "Programs & Events"
  • Direct link to registration on camp details page
  • Streamlined to 2-3 pages maximum
  • Completion rate increased to 79%

Task 2: Finding & Applying for Volunteer Opportunities

❌ BEFORE
  • Volunteer info scattered across multiple sections
  • Application process unclear and difficult to find
  • Users gave up after 3-4 unsuccessful searches
  • Only 42% found volunteer application
✅ AFTER
  • All volunteer roles grouped under "Employment/Volunteer"
  • Clear "Apply Now" buttons on each opportunity
  • One-click access from main menu
  • 88% successfully found and applied

Key Learnings

User Mental Models Matter
Card sorting revealed that users organize information differently than stakeholders assumed. Aligning with user expectations was crucial for success.
Iterate Based on Data
Two rounds of testing with different participants helped identify issues that weren't obvious in the first iteration, leading to better solutions.
Simplicity Wins
Removing redundant pages and simplifying terminology had a bigger impact than adding new features. Less is often more.

What I Would Do Differently

  • Introduce A/B testing: Would have run controlled experiments comparing old vs. new navigation to measure impact more precisely
  • Test with larger groups: More participants (20-30) per testing round would provide stronger statistical confidence in results
  • Use heatmaps: Track actual user behavior on key pages post-launch to identify remaining pain points
  • Include more diverse users: Expand testing to include more caregivers and program participants with varying technical abilities

Next Steps

📊 Monitor & Refine

Conduct quarterly usability testing to ensure navigation continues to meet user needs as content evolves

♿ WCAG Compliance

Ensure ongoing accessibility standards are maintained with regular audits and updates

🎯 Expand Testing

Include more diverse user groups to validate navigation works for all NISRA audiences

Project Summary

Through systematic UX research and information architecture improvements, I transformed NISRA's mobile navigation from confusing and cluttered to clear and intuitive.

By using card sorting, tree testing, and first-click testing with 32 participants, I created a structure aligned with user mental models, achieving an 85% user satisfaction rate and a 42% increase in task success.

Key to success: Iterative testing and data-driven decisions ensured the new structure met real user needs rather than stakeholder assumptions.
