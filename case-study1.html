The Challenge

Imagine feeling sick and you can't find a healthcare provider. How would you feel?

Patients were getting stuck before they could access care. Missing filters, unclear messaging, and confusing booking confirmation caused frustration, wasted time, and platform abandonment.

I conducted user research and recommended design improvements that reduced search time by 30%, improved messaging success by 35 points, and increased user satisfaction by 15 points.

Research Question

What barriers prevent patients from finding an available provider, contacting care teams, and completing appointment booking successfully?

Challenges

Patients were abandoning the platform due to three critical pain points:

1
Provider Search
  • No real-time filters for availability, location, or specialty
  • Outdated or vague provider info led to wasted time
  • Users gave up after failed attempts to find care
2
Communication
  • No in-app chat or video options for quick questions
  • Patients stuck playing phone tag or waiting days for replies
  • No message history or easy follow-up
3
Scheduling
  • Booking flow lacked clarity and confirmation
  • No simple reschedule/cancel option
  • No reminders caused missed or double-booked visits

  • Real-time availability filters

  • Show real-time availability and detailed provider profiles directly in search results.

1. Discovery Research

Competitor Analysis

I analyzed 3 of the most popular telehealth apps to understand how competitors support patient–provider connections.

App Communication Options Limitations
Teladoc Video call only No real-time chat; limited follow-up communication
Amwell Video call only No asynchronous messaging
MDLive Appointment-based calls Asynchronous chat and follow-up threads were inconsistent or limited by provider availability

Key Insight: Competitor platforms supported video visits, but follow-up communication was often limited, inconsistent, or separated from the booking experience. This helped us identify communication continuity as an opportunity area.

Qualitative Research

Participant Criteria

I recruited participants who had recently searched for a healthcare provider, booked an appointment online, or used a telehealth platform. The goal was to include users with different comfort levels using digital healthcare tools.

I conducted 10 in-depth user interviews (ages 22-65) via Zoom and in person, focusing on their pain points and behaviors when searching for healthcare providers and booking appointments.

Ethics Note: All research was with HIPAA-compliant protocols. Participants provided informed consent and received $50 compensation.

Key Interview Questions:

  • Walk me through the last time you searched for a healthcare provider. What was frustrating?
  • What challenges do you face when searching for a healthcare provider?
  • Tell me about your experience booking appointments online.
  • What communication tools do you use to reach providers?

Key Findings from Interviews

Finding 1: Search filters were missing

Users could not filter by specialty, location, or availability.

Finding 2: Message button was hard to find

Users had trouble finding where to message providers.

Finding 3: Booking confirmation was unclear

Users were unsure if their appointment was booked.

3. From Insights to Solutions

Design Workshop: "How Might We..."

I facilitated a design workshop to translate research insights into actionable opportunities:

HMW #1
How might we make provider availability transparent before patients invest time?
HMW #2
How might we enable quick communication without disrupting provider workflows?
HMW #3
How might we prevent scheduling conflicts and provide clear confirmations?

How Findings Informed Design Recommendations

Research Finding Design Recommendation Why It Mattered
Users could not filter by specialty, location, or availability. Add visible search filters for specialty, location, and availability. Helped users find relevant providers faster.
Users had trouble finding where to message providers. Add a visible Message Provider button on provider cards and profiles. Made communication easier to discover.
Users were unsure if appointments were successfully booked. Add a step-by-step booking flow with a clear confirmation screen. Improved trust and reduced booking confusion.
Users lacked reminders and easy follow-up options. Add email/SMS reminders, calendar options, and reschedule/cancel actions. Reduced missed appointments and gave users more control.

4. Usability Testing

I conducted two rounds of moderated usability testing with 10 participants using a Figma prototype.

Core Tasks

  • Find a provider available this week
  • Send a message to a provider
  • Schedule an appointment for a specific date and time

I measured task completion rate and time on task. After Round 1, I identified issues with hidden filters, unclear messaging entry points, and uncertainty in the booking flow. The team revised the prototype, and I ran Round 2 to validate whether the changes improved task performance.

Key Findings from Usability Testing

  • Provider Search: Provider Search: Moving availability filters from a hidden menu to a visible placement reduced search time by 30%.
  • Messaging: Adding a prominent "Message Provider" button increased success rate from 55% to 90%
  • Booking: Step-by-step appointment flow with clear confirmations improved completion from 50% to 88%

Challenges & Learnings

Challenges

Key Takeaways

Small Details Matter

Moving the filter reduced time-on-task by 30%. Never underestimate UI placement.

Actionable Insights

Framing findings as opportunities with design implications drove faster implementation.

Embrace Iteration

Round 1's 60% completion felt like failure but led to dramatic Round 2 improvements.

Closing

This project showed me how UX research can connect user pain points to measurable product improvements. Small design changes, such as making filters visible, adding a clear message button, and improving confirmation screens, had a meaningful impact on task completion and user confidence. I also learned the importance of presenting research findings in a way that connects user needs, product priorities, and operational goals.

The 15-point satisfaction increase and 30% search-time reduction helped create a faster, clearer, and more supportive path to care.
