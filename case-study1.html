Overview

I led UX research for the VirtualCare app to improve provider search, messaging, and appointment booking. Through interviews, competitive analysis, and usability testing, I identified where users were getting stuck and translated findings into design recommendations.

Results included 30% faster search time, +35 pp messaging success, +38 pp booking success, and +15 pp user satisfaction.

The Challenge

Patients were getting stuck before they could access care. Missing filters, unclear messaging, and confusing booking confirmation caused frustration, wasted time, and app abandonment.

Research Question

What barriers prevent patients from finding an available provider, contacting care teams, and completing appointment booking successfully?

3 Main Pain Points

1
Provider Search
  • No real-time filters for availability, location, or specialty
  • Outdated or vague provider info led to wasted time
  • Users gave up after failed attempts to find care
2
Messaging
  • Message button was hard to find on provider profiles
  • No conversation history or follow-up thread
  • Users defaulted to phone calls, causing delays
3
Booking
  • Booking flow lacked clarity and confirmation
  • No simple reschedule/cancel option in the app
  • No reminders caused missed or double-booked visits

Research Approach

My Role

As the UX researcher, I led the end-to-end research process: planning, participant screening, user interviews, competitive analysis, usability testing, synthesis, and stakeholder presentations. I collaborated with the design team to translate findings into prioritized recommendations.

  • Recruited & screened 10 participants (ages 22–65)
  • Conducted 10 in-depth user interviews
  • Led 2 rounds of moderated usability testing (n=10 each)
  • Analyzed qualitative & quantitative data
  • Created personas, journey maps, and findings decks
  • Presented insights to stakeholders
  • Privacy-conscious research practices, including informed consent and de-identified data

1. Discovery Research

Competitor Analysis

I analyzed 3 leading telehealth platforms to understand how competitors handle provider search, communication, and booking.

Platform TypeProvider SearchCommunicationBooking
Platform ABasic name/specialty search; no availability filtersVideo call only; no follow-up chatMulti-step booking; no confirmation clarity
Platform BSpecialty filter only; no real-time availabilityVideo call only; no asynchronous messagingConfirmation email sent but no reminders
Platform CLimited filters; outdated provider profilesAppointment-based calls; follow-up inconsistentNo reschedule/cancel option in app

Key Insight: Across platforms reviewed, provider search lacked real-time availability, communication was limited to scheduled visits with little follow-up support, and booking flows often left users uncertain about next steps. These gaps directly shaped our research focus.

Qualitative Research

Participants

10 interviews (ages 22–65), recruited via the hospital's patient outreach program. All had recently searched for a provider or used a health app. Conducted via Zoom and in person.

Ethics Note: Research followed privacy-conscious practices, including informed consent, de-identified participant data, and $50 compensation.

Key Interview Questions

  • Walk me through the last time you searched for a provider. What was frustrating?
  • Tell me about your experience booking appointments online.
  • What communication tools do you use to reach providers?

Key Findings

Finding 1: Search filters were missing

Users could not filter by specialty, location, or availability.

Finding 2: Message button was hard to find

Users struggled to locate where to message providers in the app.

Finding 3: Booking confirmation was unclear

Users were unsure if their appointment had actually been booked.

4. Usability Testing

I conducted two rounds of moderated usability testing with 10 participants using a Figma prototype.

Core Tasks

  • Find a provider available this week
  • Send a message to a provider
  • Book an appointment for a specific date and time

I measured task completion rate and time on task. After Round 1, I identified issues with hidden filters, unclear messaging entry points, and uncertainty in the booking flow. The team revised the prototype, and Round 2 validated whether the changes improved performance.

Usability testing measured completion rate and time on task (n=10). Post-launch analytics measured broader impact — see Final Results.

Key Findings

  • Search: Visible filters reduced search time by 30%.
  • Messaging: Prominent message button raised success from 55% to 90%.
  • Booking: Step-by-step flow improved completion from 50% to 88%.

Key Takeaways

Small Details Matter

Making filters more visible reduced search time and improved task completion significantly.

Actionable Insights

Connecting findings to clear design recommendations helped the team prioritize changes.

Iteration Works

Round 1 revealed friction; Round 2 showed measurable improvement after design changes.

Closing

What stood out most was how small, specific changes made the biggest difference. A visible filter, a clearly labeled message button, a confirmation screen — none of these were complex to build, but each one removed a moment of confusion that was causing real patients to give up on getting care.

If I continued this work, I would research the provider side — understanding how care teams experience the same flows would help design a system that works for both ends of the conversation.
